Plumbing in an old timber villa: what to know

Plumbing in an old timber villa in Jūrmala has to be planned differently from a brick house.

Before replacing pipes, find out whether the villa is a monument or lies in an urban planning monument area: then a rebuild that may alter the building needs written permission. Pipes in timber walls are better joined without a flame, since for work with a flame the Fire Safety Regulations require combustible materials within 5 m to be cleared or protected and the place to be watched for another four hours after the work.

Why a timber villa is special: hollow walls, damp and protection

In the Jūrmala territorial plan data (around 2010) the city had 558 architectural monuments and 11 urban planning monument areas, and most of the protected historic housing was timber dwellings. For example, Liedaga iela 7, 18 and 20 in Dubulti are typical single-storey timber summer houses with verandas and carved woodwork.

In a timber house the pipes run through hollow walls and intermediate floors, where there may be dry wood, old shavings or insulation. The Fire Safety Regulations prohibit welding, cutting, soldering and heating hollow structures without making sure of their contents (point 314.1.5).

The other is damp. Jūrmala’s conditions for historic interiors advise avoiding sharp swings in humidity and temperature. A small leak under a bath can stay unseen for a long time in a timber floor, because wood soaks up water.

What you can check yourself without risk

Find out the building’s status. If the villa is a monument of national importance or stands in an area of national importance, for example the Dubulti-Majori-Dzintari-Bulduri-Lielupe summer-house district (No. 6083), repair permission is issued by the National Heritage Board. In a local-importance monument area, ask the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre whether the work you plan could alter the building.

Jūrmala’s conditions require owners of historic buildings to survey the building regularly, especially the roof, rainwater drainage elements, facades and foundations. For plumbing this means: look under the bath and the sinks to see whether the floor is dark or soft, and in the basement or crawl space whether the pipes are damp.

For exposed pipes in basements, crawl spaces and attics, the City of Toronto, for example, advises insulating them with foam sleeves. In an old summer house with a veranda these stretches usually freeze first in winter.

What the law and Jūrmala's rules say

Article 21 of the law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments” states that cultural monuments of national and regional importance may be repaired only with the written permission of the National Heritage Board, and monuments of local importance with the permission of the local authority.

In Jūrmala’s local-importance urban planning monument area, repair and rebuilding of a cultural monument or historic building that may alter it may be done only with the written permission of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre, and the owner pays for the work. Rebuilding a building in these areas requires a design brief, which is not needed for buildings without cultural and historical value or buildings built after 1940.

The Fire Safety Regulations treat work with an open flame or sparks as fire-hazardous work. The work area within a 5 m radius must be cleared of combustible materials or they must be protected (points 310 and 311), and a temporary work place must be watched for at least four hours after the work ends (point 306).

The law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments”, Article 21; the conditions for Jūrmala’s urban planning monument areas; the Fire Safety Regulations, points 2.13, 306, 310, 311 and 314.1.5 (likumi.lv, checked 19 November 2026).

When it is for the authorities and when for the plumber

The permission and the design brief are the owner’s matter: for monuments of national importance with the National Heritage Board, in local-importance areas with the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre, not the plumber’s. The plumber can say which jobs touch walls, floors and the facade, so that you know what to ask.

Once it is clear what is allowed, the plumber replaces the pipes and connections. Repiping a whole house is from €500, replacing a single stretch from €10/m, labour price, materials not included. During the call before the work you go through the job, the type of joints and the price.

After the work, update the house documents. Jūrmala’s regulations (points 32 and 33) require water and sewer diagrams and as-built drawings with pipe diameters, depths, fittings and meter assemblies to be kept, and in an old villa a new route without a drawing is hard to find later behind the cladding.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Can pipes be soldered next to a timber wall?

The Fire Safety Regulations treat soldering as fire-hazardous work: combustible materials within 5 m must be cleared or protected, the place watched for at least four hours, and heating hollow structures without making sure of their contents is prohibited. So in a timber house joints without a flame are chosen.

Does a Dubulti summer house with a veranda need permission for a new bathroom?

It depends on the building’s status and on whether the work alters the building. Dubulti lies in the area of the urban planning monument of national importance No. 6083, so before the work ask the National Heritage Board; in local-importance areas ask the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre.

Why do pipes in a villa's basement and attic need insulating?

There is usually no heating there, and the pipes freeze there first. For example, the City of Toronto advises insulating exposed pipes in basements, crawl spaces, garages and attics with foam sleeves.
