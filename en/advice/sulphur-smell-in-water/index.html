Water or water heater smells of hydrogen sulphide - why and what to do

Water or water heater smells of hydrogen sulphide, that is, of rotten eggs?

If only the hot water smells, the cause is usually in the water heater: in the tank, bacteria together with the action of the anode produce the gas, and cleaning the water heater and a different type of anode often help. If the cold water smells too, it can only be established with a water analysis; the Ķemeri sulphur springs are a natural feature, not the town water supply.

Why hot water smells: the anode, sulphates and bacteria

The trade magazine Water Technology explains that usually only the hot water smells. Sulphate-reducing bacteria use the hydrogen produced in the water heater by the anode’s protective action; the greater the anode current, the more gas.

Sulphates are also in Jūrmala’s tap water, but below the norm of 250 mg/l. In the 2026 average values of Jūrmala’s water utility “Jūrmalas ūdens” (as of 19 August 2026) they are 210 mg/l in Dzintari, 170 mg/l in Jaundubulti and 160 mg/l in Kauguri and Ķemeri.

The Lochinvar water heater instructions state that the harmless bacteria usually present in tap water can multiply in a water heater and cause a rotten-egg smell. Water Technology adds that any water heater left unused for a long time can start to smell, because bacteria multiply in a standing tank. So in a summer house the smell can appear after winter, when the water heater has not been used.

The Ķemeri sulphur springs and the town water supply

The Nature Conservation Agency explains that around Ķemeri there are about 30 sulphur springs in a strip 700 m long, and the sulphur waters form deep under the bog. Hydrogen sulphide arises in the gypsum-rich Salaspils aquifer, where sulphate-reducing bacteria produce it if the peat layer is thicker than 1.5 m.

Ķemeri’s centralised water supply uses the deepest, Devonian Gauja aquifer, and the water is treated to remove iron and manganese before it enters the network. A 2016 report assesses these aquifers as well protected. According to the agency, the smell of the sulphur springs is noticeable only in the immediate vicinity of the outflow points, especially in summer.

Checks you can safely make yourself

Open one tap, first on cold and then on hot water, and compare the smell. If only the hot water smells, the fault is in the water heater or the hot water pipes. If both smell, the question is about the water itself or the pipes in the house.

After a long absence the Health Inspectorate recommends running both the cold and the hot water: open the tap only slightly at first so that no aerosol forms, and increase the flow gradually to full within ten minutes. If the hot water still smells afterwards, the problem is in the tank.

Do not open the water heater’s cover, anode or heating element yourself: a water heater is an electrical appliance under pressure.

How water is measured in Jūrmala and how to check your own

Routine monitoring in Jūrmala provides for 29 samples a month sent to an accredited laboratory, and in 2026 an additional 1,320 internal monitoring samples are planned. “Jūrmalas ūdens” publishes sulphates, iron and hardness; if you notice a smell at the tap, ask the company or a laboratory for a water analysis.

The company does not add chlorine or fluorides to drinking water. If the cold water smells, ask “Jūrmalas ūdens” about measurements at your station, or take a sample of your tap water to an accredited laboratory.

“Jūrmalas ūdens” drinking water quality and frequently asked questions (jurmalasudens.lv); Nature Conservation Agency, LSM, 08.04.2024. Checked 19 November 2026.

When it is the water utility's job and when the plumber's

If the cold water smells at all taps, it is a question for “Jūrmalas ūdens” or a laboratory. If only the hot water smells, it is a water heater job: the plumber drains it, cleans it and checks the anode. A water heater clean-out with an anode check is from €75, labour price, materials not included.

There are several options for the anode. Lochinvar mentions a special zinc anode, Water Technology an aluminium-zinc anode (92% aluminium, 7% zinc), which reduces the anode current and often lessens the smell, but with softened water it often does not work. A third option is a powered anode, which is plugged into a socket and feeds a milliamp current into the tank through an electrode with a titanium coating.

To reduce the smell Lochinvar permits heating the tank to 60 °C, but above 49 °C the risk of scalding rises, so it recommends thermostatic mixing valves at the outlets.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Does water from the Ķemeri sulphur springs come out of the tap?

No. Ķemeri’s water supply uses the deepest, Devonian Gauja aquifer, which a 2016 report assesses as well protected, while hydrogen sulphide forms in the higher Salaspils aquifer under the bogs.

Can a water heater be run without an anode so that it does not smell?

No. Lochinvar warns that the anode has to stay in the water heater at all times. The smell is reduced by a different type of anode or by cleaning, not by taking the anode out.

Is chlorine added to Jūrmala's water?

No. “Jūrmalas ūdens” states that neither chlorine nor fluorides are added to drinking water. Total iron in the treated water is 0.02–0.07 mg/l.
