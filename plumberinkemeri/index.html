Plumber in Ķemeri - a sulphur spring resort with its own water station

A plumber in Ķemeri works in the western part of Jūrmala, 44 km from Riga, in a resort whose territory was long tied to the Duchy of Courland.

The whole Ķemeri part lies in the landscape protection and neutral zone of Ķemeri National Park, and Jūrmala’s smallest water station operates here. So before larger work in Ķemeri you need to know both the building’s status and whether the house takes town water or its own.

The station, Vēršupīte park and the 1929 water tower

The Ķemeri station building was built in 1922 to a design by Artūrs Mēdlingers, the “Ķemeri” hotel opened in 1936, and the Lutheran church was consecrated in 1897. Most of Jūrmala’s protected historic development is wooden dwelling houses. In such a house, soldering or heating near hollow walls and intermediate floors is allowed only after their contents have been checked (Fire Safety Regulations, point 314.1.5).

Ķemeri park was laid out along the Vēršupīte, and it holds the “Ķirzaciņa” spring with its pavilion and the Love Islet rotunda (1928, architect Frīdrihs Skujiņš). Around Ķemeri there are about 30 sulphur springs in a strip 700 m long. Hydrogen sulphide forms in the gypsum-rich Salaspils water horizon under the bogs, while the town water main draws its water from deeper down; more on the page water smells of sulphur.

The water tower was built in 1929 to a design by Frīdrihs Skujiņš, renovated in 2021 and still serves water supply; its viewing platforms are at 12 and 42 m. In the same year two artesian boreholes were drilled in Ķemeri and a water main was built, and this began the centralised water supply of the whole of Jūrmala. In phase I of the water utility programme the tower was reconstructed, and in phase II the Ķemeri water treatment plant was completely rebuilt.

“Ķemeri resort” and the national park zone before digging

Ķemeri has the state-protected urban planning monument “Ķemeri resort”. The territorial plan sets that in the national park’s landscape protection and neutral zone, building designs and the choice of building sites are agreed with the park administration. Article 21 of the law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments” allows repair of a monument of national importance only with written permission from the National Heritage Board.

Changing a tap, toilet or water heater inside a room is usually not a building design. A new yard service pipe, an outlet through the foundations of a historic building or a pipe route along a facade can be one, and before such work ask the municipality what approval is needed.

The law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments”, Article 21; Fire Safety Regulations, points 306 and 314.1.5; Jūrmala City Council binding regulations No. 38, points 13 and 22. Checked 19 November 2026.

The Devonian horizon, 191 m³ a day and the phase III networks

Ķemeri’s centralised water supply uses the deepest Devonian Gauja water horizon, and in the 2016 data the water is treated to remove iron and manganese before it enters the network. These horizons are sealed from above by Quaternary clay loam, and a 2016 report rates them as well protected. The Ķemeri station is the smallest of Jūrmala’s four stations: 191 m³ a day.

Some users also take drinking water from the shallower Quaternary sand layers, that is, from their own wells. LBN 222-15 (point 150) forbids joining a drinking water system with a system whose water does not meet drinking water requirements. In a house with two sources this is the main plumbing rule, and the Jūrmala regulations spell it out (see the work below).

In 2010, 77% of Ķemeri residents used town water, but only 36% had centralised sewerage. In November 2015 the phase III networks were handed over: 6.7 km of water main, 13.3 km of gravity sewer, 429 sewer connections and 3 pumping stations. For phase IV, networks were designed in 2018 for Katedrāles, Liesmas, Medus, Pētera Pāvila, Stropu, Tukuma and Ziedoņa iela, and in 2020 the construction contract “Asari - Ķemeri” was signed.

Pipes in an old resort house and separating two water sources

Replacing pipes in a private house and an old villa. “Repiping a whole flat or house” costs from €500 per property, and a single section, “Replacing water pipe”, from €10/m; labour price, materials not included. If soldering is still needed, the hot work site must be watched for at least four hours after the work ends (point 306). In a wooden house this is a practical reason to choose joints made without a flame.

Connection to the town networks. “Yard connection to the town network” is charged per metre, and the price is set by length, depth, restoring the surface and permits. If the old well stays, Jūrmala binding regulations No. 38 (point 22) require an air gap of at least 50 mm or a special non-return valve at the junction. After the work the owner confirms that their own well or driven well is not connected to the centralised system (point 13).

Often asked in Jūrmala

Does the smell of the Ķemeri sulphur springs get into the house water supply?

The Nature Conservation Agency notes that the smell of sulphur water is noticeable only close to where it emerges, especially in summer, and does not spread to the wider surroundings. The town water main uses the deeper Devonian Gauja horizon, not the Salaspils horizon of the sulphur springs. If you still notice a smell in the water, ask Jūrmalas ūdens about a water analysis; more in the article on the hydrogen sulphide smell.

Our Ķemeri house has its own well. Can we keep it after connecting?

The Jūrmala regulations cover the case where a building has two sources: at the junction there must be an air gap of at least 50 mm or a special non-return valve (binding regulations No. 38, point 22). The well must not be connected directly to the town water main, and the owner confirms this after the work. Connecting to the phase IV network is not compulsory.

Ķemeri is 44 km from Riga. Does the trip cost extra?

Ķemeri is one of the places we work, and the published labour price already includes getting to Ķemeri. “On-site inspection” (from €40 per visit) is the price when the plumber comes, inspects and does no other work. All the published labour prices are on the price page.
