Trap replacement - smell, leak and water seal depth

A trap replacement in Jūrmala is usually ordered in two cases: water leaks under a sink or bath, or a sewer smell comes from the drain.

In April in Majori, opening a rented flat after winter

“Jūrmalas ūdens” (Jūrmala’s water utility) writes that the number of residents and guests in Jūrmala changes considerably between summer and winter, and the load on the sewerage depends on that. In a rented flat in Majori that has not been used all winter, you notice it at the drain in April.

The manufacturer Wavin explains that in an appliance left idle over a long period the water in the trap evaporates, the seal disappears, and a sewer smell appears in the room. How quickly this happens depends on air temperature and humidity. Among such places Wavin also names the floor drain, which gets water less often than a sink.

After a long absence the Health Inspectorate recommends running both the cold and the hot water through, and that also refills the traps. How to do it is in the first question below.

Smell and wipes: assumptions and facts

Often assumed: if a drain smells, the trap is at fault and has to be replaced.

In fact: after a long absence the smell is often only from evaporated water, and running the water is enough. If the smell stays even though the appliance is used every day, the trap, its seal or its connection to the drain may be damaged.

Often assumed: wet wipes marked “flushable” do no harm to a trap or the sewerage.

In fact: at the Sloka wastewater treatment plants 14 tonnes of waste are screened out a year, and “Jūrmalas ūdens” reminds people that wet wipes, including those with this marking, belong in the rubbish (LV portāls, 2026).

A wipe does not dissolve in water like paper. It catches in the bend of the trap or further along the pipe, so after wipes the blockage is often found exactly where the trap joins the drain.

Water seal: 50 mm, 30 mm and a 25 mm minimum

The water seal is the column of water in the trap that stops sewer air getting into the room. The ACO ShowerDrain S+ DN 50 floor drain comes in two versions: with a 50 mm and with a 30 mm water seal (EN 1253-1). The shallower one is chosen where there is little room in the floor.

In Great Britain, for example, the building regulations (Approved Document H) require a working trap to keep at least 25 mm of water. With a 30 mm seal the margin to that limit is only 5 mm, so a shallow trap in a rarely used shower dries out faster than a deep one.

Replacing a sink and bath trap: a 50 mm seal and hair under the bath

For its sink trap the manufacturer Geberit states a water seal depth of 50 mm, and the trap is quality-controlled to EN 274-3. When replacing a sink trap, compare this figure with the description of the new trap: the deeper the seal, the longer it lasts in a rarely used sink.

A bath or shower trap cannot easily be cleaned without removing the tray; Geberit recommends pulling the hair out with long tweezers. If the trap still smells afterwards or water leaks under the bath, it is replaced, and the labour price is determined mainly by access under the bath.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Why is a tap opened slowly after a long absence?

So that no aerosol forms. The Health Inspectorate recommends starting with a small stream and increasing it gradually to full within ten minutes, for both cold and hot water. That way the traps fill up in rarely used places too.

Where does what is washed down a sink in Jūrmala end up?

Jūrmala’s sewerage system is 435.6 km long with 55 pumping stations. About 73% of the waste water is treated in Sloka, about 25% is pumped from the Lielupe system to Riga’s Daugavgrīva treatment plant.

What cleaning products must not be poured into Jūrmala's drains?

Jūrmala binding regulations No. 38 (sub-point 45.4) prohibit discharging acids and substances that can release gases dangerous to people, including hydrogen sulphide.
