Laying underfloor heating loops - permit, pressure test, slow start

Laying underfloor heating loops in a historic Jūrmala villa starts with a question to the municipality, not with pipes: in a monument area, rebuilding that may alter the building may be carried out only with written permission.

In a new build in Piņķi or Babīte, the first step is a pressure test of the loops before the screed. The price depends on the area, the manifold and the floor construction.

In September, when the old floor is opened in a Melluži summer house

Melluži falls within an urban planning monument of both national and local importance, which covers the summer-house district from Vaivari to Jaundubulti. The value of the national monument is the historic street grid and the division of land plots.

In such an area, repair and rebuilding of a historic building that may alter it may be carried out only with the written permission of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre, and the owner pays for the work. Opening the floor across a whole room and a manifold cabinet in the wall may be exactly that kind of work, so ask about it before starting.

In September people usually no longer live in a summer house, and the floor can be opened without hurry. The permit question is still settled in summer, because the work does not start without it, and after the screed the floor still needs time so that it can be started up slowly.

Permit and the first heat-up: assumptions and facts

Often assumed: for a historic villa, the permit is always given by the municipality.

In fact: Article 21 of the law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments” says that cultural monuments of national and regional importance may be repaired only with the written permission of the National Heritage Board. Monuments of local importance need the municipality’s permission. Ask the municipality which case applies to your house.

Often assumed: the new floor can be switched straight on to full output so that the screed dries faster.

In fact: Jūrmala’s conditions for historic interiors ask for sudden swings in humidity and temperature to be avoided. For its own system, the manufacturer Myson requires the screed to be left to cure for at least 21 days and then to start from 30 °C, adding 3 °C each day.

The law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments”, Article 21; conditions of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre (likumi.lv); Myson Floortec installation instructions; checked on 19 November 2026.

Pressure test: 3 bar for 30 minutes, 1 bar during the screed

Before the screed, the loops are tested under pressure. The Myson Floortec instructions require the loops to be held at 3 bar for 30 minutes and then left at 1 bar in the pipes during screeding.

The pressure stays in the pipes while people walk on them and the screed is poured. If a pipe is damaged in that time, the gauge shows it while the pipe is still visible, not after it lies under the screed.

For other manufacturers' systems, the test pressure and time are set by their instructions or the design. If there is a design, the loops are laid and tested to it.

Water underfloor heating in a flood-risk district: manifold above the floor

In the Lielupe flood risk plan, the protected summer-house districts from Dubulti to the Lielupe and from Vaivari to Jaundubulti are also exposed to floods, as are the Kauguri fishing village and the historic centre of Sloka.

BALTA states that floods damage basements and heating equipment most, and advises placing them at least 10–20 cm above the floor. In underfloor heating, this applies to the manifold and the mixing unit, if there is one: they are not fitted right at the floor in a basement or a low plant room.

The manifold’s place is chosen before the floor is opened, because it decides where all the loops will run. After the screed it is no longer moved.

Underfloor heating installation cost: a 40 m² living room in a Melluži villa

In a Melluži villa, underfloor heating is laid in a 40 m² living room. The number of loops is set by the design or the manufacturer’s calculation, and each loop needs its own outlet on the manifold, so the manifold’s size depends on the number of loops, not on the area alone.

Laying underfloor heating loops is priced per m², and the area, the manifold and the floor construction set the price. In the hall, where the floor is not opened, a radiator stays, and it is replaced with a new one: changing one radiator is from €70 per radiator. In total at least €70 plus the floor by area; labour price, materials not included.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Does a house built after 1940 in a monument area need a design brief?

By Jūrmala’s conditions, a design brief in urban planning monument areas is not needed for buildings built after 1940 and buildings without cultural-historical value. For a particular house, ask the municipality.

May antifreeze be poured into a summer house's underfloor heating?

In a heating circuit it may, if the product is meant for it. For example, the manufacturer of Sentinel X500 requires a dose of at least 20% and prohibits its use in drinking-water systems. Underfloor heating is a heating circuit, and tap water does not mix with it.

Is fitting underfloor heating in a timber villa hot work?

The Fire Safety Regulations treat work with an open flame or sparks as hot work (point 2.13). If the pipes are joined without a flame, it is not; if they are soldered, the work area within a 5 m radius is cleared of combustible materials or those are protected (points 310 and 311).
