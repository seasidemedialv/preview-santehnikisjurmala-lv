Replacing radiators in a timber house - flame, 5 m and four hours

Replacing a radiator in a Jūrmala timber summer house or villa starts with fire safety: if the supply pipe is cut with sparks or soldered with a flame, that is hot work with its own requirements.

When a radiator is changed in a single-storey Dubulti summer house with a veranda

Dubulti is one of the old fishing villages with an irregular street grid, and Liedaga iela 7, 18 and 20 are typical single-storey timber summer houses of Dubulti with verandas and carved woodwork. In Jūrmala’s territorial plan data (around 2010), most of the protected historic buildings are exactly timber dwellings.

In such a house, changing a radiator is not only a question of size and pipes. Point 2.13 of the Fire Safety Regulations treats work with an open flame or sparks as hot work. A timber wall, floor and veranda are usually closer than 5 m to the radiator.

So before the work you decide how the old radiator will be disconnected and which connections will join the new one. You go through this on the phone, when you describe the house and the wall material.

5 m radius and hollow walls: points 310, 311 and 314.1.5

Points 310 and 311 of the Fire Safety Regulations require the hot-work area within a 5 m radius to be cleared of combustible materials or those materials to be protected from catching fire. In a summer house these are curtains, furniture, floor coverings and the timber finish itself.

Point 314.1.5 of the regulations prohibits welding, cutting, soldering and heating hollow structures without making sure of their contents. This also applies to the walls and intermediate floors of timber buildings, where the supply pipes pass through.

So in a timber house, joints that need no flame suit better, for example press-fit or screwed ones. Such a joint can also be made next to a timber wall without working with an open flame.

Four hours after soldering: point 306

If a joint is soldered after all, point 306 of the regulations requires the temporary hot-work site to be watched for at least four hours after the work ends. In a timber house, these four hours are part of the working day’s plan.

This is one more reason why, in a historic timber house, the type of supply connection is chosen before the work, not at the moment when the old radiator is already off the wall.

Permit and thermostatic head: two assumptions

Often assumed: in any old Jūrmala house, changing a radiator needs a design project.

In fact: in urban planning monument areas, a design brief is needed for rebuilding a building, but not for buildings without cultural-historical value and buildings built after 1940. Repair that may alter a historic building needs the written permission of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre.

Often assumed: the new radiator can safely be hidden behind a cupboard or sofa.

In fact: Oventrop states that the thermostat or its remote sensor must not be covered by curtains or furniture. If the radiator stays behind a piece of furniture, the thermostatic head gets a remote sensor or the radiator is moved.

Installing radiators in a private house, moving them and replacing heating pipes

In the old fishing villages, for example Dubulti, Vecbulduri, Asari and Kauguri, houses usually combine a family home with a summer house. When such a house starts to be lived in all year round, installing radiators in a private house means choosing places by the room and by the pipe route to the boiler.

Moving a radiator to another wall and replacing the heating pipes up to the radiator in a timber house pass through the same structures that point 314.1.5 applies to. So the pipe route and the type of joints are decided together with the radiator’s position.

Worked example: a radiator behind a cupboard in Dubulti

In the bedroom of a Dubulti summer house, a radiator must be replaced, and a built-in cupboard stands in front of it. The radiator stays where it is, but the thermostatic head gets a remote sensor, placed in the room.

Changing one radiator is from €70 per radiator. Fitting the head with a remote sensor and running the sensor cable, if it takes two hours, is hourly work from €25/h. In total at least €120 for the job; the radiator, the head and the connections come on top.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Can the thermostatic head setting be limited on a new radiator in a rented flat?

It can. Oventrop states that the head’s setting can be limited or locked with limiter pins, so a guest cannot turn it all the way.

Does changing a radiator in a summer-house district that is a monument need the Cultural Space Centre's permission?

A permit is needed for repair and rebuilding of a historic building that may alter the building. Whether changing the radiator or the pipe route in your house is such work, ask the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre before the work.

Does the ban on hollow structures also apply to a timber floor?

Yes. Point 314.1.5 of the Fire Safety Regulations prohibits welding, cutting, soldering and heating hollow structures without making sure of their contents, and it also applies to timber walls and intermediate floors.
