In a basement you first have to decide whether the waste runs to the street by itself or with a pump, and which closure will stop the street network’s waste water from coming back.

In the basement of a Sloka detached house, where a shower and toilet are wanted

Sloka lies in the western part of Jūrmala on the left bank of the Lielupe, and its part of the city is mostly dense detached-house development. In river floodplains and dunes' dips, Jūrmala typically has a high groundwater level, 0.5–2.0 m.

If a basin, toilet, shower or floor drain is fitted in a basement, point 34.4 of Jūrmala binding regulations No. 38 requires automatic closures or gate valves in the sewerage. They stop the street network’s waste water from flowing into the basement through the new outlet.

So a basement bathroom starts not with tiles but with the question of where the waste goes: with a fall to the street sewer, or upwards with a pump.

Groundwater at a depth of 0.5–2.0 m means that the depth of the floor drain and the waste outlet is chosen according to whether the basement floor is above or below this level.

Requirements for basements and water sources: Jūrmala City Council binding regulations No. 38 of 25 July 2024, points 13, 22, 34.4, 45.8, 45.9, 53 and 59; viewed on likumi.lv on 19 November 2026.

Valves, venting and storage: three assumptions about basement outlets

Often assumed: valves on the new branches are unnecessary, because they will be tiled over.

In fact: point 53 of regulations No. 38 states that the user is responsible for the consequences if the system lacks shut-off fittings or rodding eyes are not hermetically closed. An accessible valve on each new branch is cheaper than an opened wall.

Often assumed: an air valve on the venting pipe is enough for a lifting unit.

In fact: the Saniflo SANICUBIC instructions require the unit to be vented with an outlet at roof level, and the ventilation must be open in both directions. Air admittance valves, that is, mechanical valves, are not permitted.

Often assumed: winter stores can be kept in the basement next to the new shower.

In fact: point 59 of regulations No. 38 forbids storing foodstuffs and valuables in basements where sanitary appliances and drainage systems have been installed.

A toilet in the basement: black water and an anti-backflow loop

A toilet in a basement is a source of black water, so the valve must be type 3, for example the ACO Quatrix. If the waste is lower than the street sewer, a lifting unit pumps the waste upwards, and the Saniflo SANICUBIC instructions require the rising main to have an anti-backflow loop above the maximum backflow level, which is the street level, unless local requirements say otherwise.

During a power cut the connected appliances must not be used.

Repairing the unit is covered on the basement pumps page, while the toilet in its new place is charged as toilet fitting, from €45.

An automatic closure for a basement drain: EN 13564 types

Manufacturers classify backwater valves by the standard EN 13564. The ACO Junior (type 5) and Triplex (type 2) are intended only for grey water, so they are not suitable at a toilet.

Grey water comes from a basin and shower, black water from a toilet. The valve type is decided before the new outlets are tiled over.

If there is no non-return valve, the user is responsible for the consequences (point 53 of regulations No. 38), and in a basement below street level this applies to every new waste outlet.

Worked total for a Sloka basement: a toilet with a lifting unit and a backwater valve

In the basement of a Sloka detached house a toilet is fitted with a lifting unit. Two new outlets are needed: a cold water outlet for the toilet cistern and a waste outlet where the unit’s rising main joins the house’s sewerage.

Swapping or fitting a toilet is from €45, an extra outlet from €35 per point, so €45 and 2 × €35 together is at least €115. The price of fitting the lifting unit and the backwater valve is given after an inspection, and the devices themselves and the pipes are extra.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Does a shower fitted in a Sloka basement need an automatic closure in the sewerage?

Yes. Point 34.4 of Jūrmala binding regulations No. 38 requires automatic closures or gate valves in the sewerage if the basement has sanitary appliances or drainage.

May a basement drainage pump be connected to the new waste outlet?

No. It is forbidden to discharge rain, melted snow and plot drainage water into Jūrmala’s centralised sewerage (points 45.8 and 45.9 of regulations No. 38). Most of Jūrmala has a separate stormwater sewerage, and the Engineering Structures Division of the municipality’s Development Department is responsible for it.

May I keep my own well after connecting to the town water supply?

Point 13 of regulations No. 38 requires confirmation that your own well or driven well is not connected to the centralised system. If the well is used, for example, for the garden, it stays separate, and where the two sources still meet, a 50 mm air gap or a special non-return valve is needed (point 22).
