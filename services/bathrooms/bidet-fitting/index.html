Bidet fitting - a connection to the villa's hot water loop

Bidet fitting in Dzintari and Bulduri villas often means joining the bidet to the hot water circulation loop, so that in the long pipe to a rarely used bidet the water does not stand at a warmth where legionella bacteria multiply.

In the Dzintari summer house quarter, when a villa's bathroom is replanned

Dzintari grew up on the land of the Bulduri manor as a prestigious district of detached houses for wealthy summer residents, and the first plots were entered in the land register in 1871. “Dzintaru-Bulduru vasarnīcu kvartāli” is a separate urban planning monument No. 6084.

In a large villa the bathroom may be at the other end of the house from the water heater or heat unit. The hot water runs to the bidet through a long pipe, and cools in it between uses. A bidet is used briefly and not every day, so the water stands in this pipe longer than in a kitchen.

Before the work you also need to know the status of the house. Jūrmala’s conditions in urban planning monument areas require a project design brief for the rebuilding of a building, but not for buildings without cultural and historical value and not for buildings built after 1940.

Permission and hot water temperature: two assumptions about the bidet

Often assumed: in a historic villa an internal bathroom rebuild never needs permission.

In fact: in a Jūrmala urban planning monument area of local importance, repair and rebuilding of a cultural monument or historic building that may alter it is allowed only with the written permission of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre. Connecting a bidet to existing supplies does not usually alter a building. If the work affects walls, ceilings or the facade, ask the centre.

Often assumed: to avoid scalding at the bidet, the hot water throughout the house should be kept cooler.

In fact: the Health Inspectorate states that the technically simplest way to limit legionella is a constantly high hot water temperature. A safe temperature at the bidet is given by its mixer tap, not by a lower temperature in the water heater.

Circulation loop to the bidet: 50–60 °C up to the draw-off point

ECDC explains that legionella bacteria multiply in standing water at 20–50 °C, in pipes with low flow too. In a long pipe to a rarely used bidet, the water cools to exactly this range between uses.

For example, ECDC advises tourist accommodation to keep the hot water circulating at 50–60 °C throughout the system, right up to the draw-off points. In a villa that means a loop along which the hot water returns from the bathroom to the water heater, and the bidet supply is joined to this loop, not to a long branch that ends at the bidet.

If the loop ends on the ground floor, it is extended to the bathroom: the hot water pipe goes up, and beside it a second, return pipe comes back. Where the two will run depends on whether the villa’s walls and ceilings may be opened, and that is again a question of permission.

If the bidet is in a guest bathroom that is rarely used, another ECDC recommendation for tourist accommodation helps: in unoccupied rooms, let every tap and shower run for a few minutes, at least weekly, until the water reaches the stated temperatures.

Bidet mixer in water of 3.21–4.25 mmol/l: limescale and rubber seals

Among the places where legionella bacteria multiply, ECDC also lists limescale and rubber seals. A bidet mixer and its hoses have both.

The total hardness of Jūrmala’s water is 3.21–4.25 mmol/l, and according to the WHO document, water above 1.8 mmol/l is already very hard. “Jūrmalas ūdens” does not plan to supply moderately hard or soft water, so additional softening is up to the consumers themselves.

In Dzintari the water is prepared by the city station with iron removal and nanofiltration to remove sulphates, but the treated water still has calcium at 85–98 mg/l.

ECDC’s 15-point plan for tourist accommodation also includes keeping taps clean of limescale. So the bidet mixer is fitted so that its spout and the hose connections stay accessible for cleaning.

Worked total for a Dzintari villa: bidet and 12 m of loop pipe

In a Dzintari villa the bidet goes into a first-floor bathroom. The water heater is in the basement, and the hot water circulation loop ends on the ground floor. In place of the old 6 m hot water pipe a new one goes up to the first floor, and beside it 6 m of return pipe.

Replacing a water pipe is from €10/m, so for 12 m of pipe at least €120. The price of connecting a bidet depends on whether the supply pipes are already in place or have to be run, on the fixing and on materials, and is given after an inspection. The bidet, mixer tap and pipes are extra.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Does a villa built after 1940 need a project design brief before a bathroom rebuild?

Jūrmala’s conditions in urban planning monument areas require it for the rebuilding of a building, but not for buildings built after 1940 and not for buildings without cultural and historical value. If in doubt, ask the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre.

At what temperature must hot water circulate up to the bidet?

For example, ECDC advises tourist accommodation to keep the hot water circulating at 50–60 °C throughout the system, and the cold water below 20 °C, right up to the draw-off points. In standing water below 50 °C, legionella bacteria multiply.

How does fitting a hygiene shower by the toilet follow point 150 of LBN 222-15?

The shower head can fall into the toilet and stay in the water, but point 150 of LBN 222-15 forbids joining a drinking water system to a system in which the water does not meet drinking water requirements. So the holder is placed so that the hose does not reach the water in the toilet, and the shower valve is closed after use.
