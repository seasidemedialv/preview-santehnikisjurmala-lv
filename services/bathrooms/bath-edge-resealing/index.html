Bath resealing - the timber floor under the bath and the limescale edge

Bath resealing in Jūrmala’s timber houses protects the floor first: water that for years seeps through a loose joint between the bath and the wall reaches the boards and joists.

In a Dubulti timber summer house, when the floor under the bath goes soft

Liedaga iela 7, 18 and 20 are typical single-storey timber summer houses of Dubulti with verandas and wood carving. According to Jūrmala territorial plan data, the city had 558 architectural monuments, and most of the protected historic development is timber residential buildings.

In a timber house, water that seeps through a cracked joint between the bath and the wall reaches the floorboards and joists. The first sign is often a soft floor by the bath or a dark patch under it, not a puddle.

So in such a house the joint is worth replacing as soon as it comes away, not when the floor already has to be repaired. In a summer house that is not lived in over winter, it is worth looking at the joint before the season and checking with a finger whether it still holds to the bath edge.

Chlorine and a historic interior: two assumptions about the joint

Often assumed: the joint turns yellow or brittle from chlorine in the tap water.

In fact: “Jūrmalas ūdens” does not add chlorine or fluorides to drinking water. The joint is damaged by dampness, movement between the bath and the wall, and cleaning products, not by chlorine from the tap.

Often assumed: dampness in a bathroom of an old villa is only a bathroom matter.

In fact: Jūrmala’s conditions require owners of historic buildings to inspect the building regularly and to avoid sharp fluctuations of humidity and temperature in historic interiors. A sound joint and a closed drain are part of that.

Hardness from 2.33 to 4.0 mmol/l in one sample: a white edge on the joint

In the “Jūrmalas ūdens” sample of 22 May 2026, total hardness was from 2.33 mmol/l in Ķemeri to 4.0 mmol/l in Jaundubulti. The 2026 average iron concentration in the treated water was 9.7–16.0 µg/l against a norm of 200 µg/l.

There is little iron, but a lot of calcium and magnesium. When water dries on the joint, a white limescale edge is left, and in harder water it appears sooner. This edge does not yet mean the joint leaks, and it is enough to clean it with a product that is suitable for silicone.

The joint is replaced when it comes away from the bath or the wall, when it has cracks, or when black spots are under the silicone. The new joint is run only after the old silicone has been removed completely and the surface degreased, and the bath is filled first, so that the joint cures once the bath has already settled.

Soft boards under the bath: when the joint is no longer the whole job

If, on inspection, the floor by the bath gives under the foot or the boards are dark, water has been there for a long time. A new joint on a moving floor will come away again, because the bath moves together with the floor.

Then the order is this: the bath is lifted out, a carpenter repairs the floor, the bath is put back and connected, and only then is the new joint run. Repairing the floor is not plumbing work, and you plan it separately.

While the bath is lifted out, it is a good moment to inspect the bath’s drain and overflow too, because they can also be a source of dampness under the floor.

Worked total for a Dubulti summer house: soft boards under the bath

In a Dubulti timber summer house, a joint replacement is requested, but on inspection the floor under the bath edge is soft. The joint is not run on that visit, because the floor has to be repaired first. If no other work is done, the on-site inspection is from €40 per visit.

Once the floor is repaired, lifting out the bath and connecting it back is discussed during the call. Taking out the old bath and connecting the new one in the price list is from €70 per bath. Often asked in Jūrmala

Does the joint get less limescale in Ķemeri than in Jaundubulti?

In the Ķemeri sample the hardness was lower, but Ķemeri’s water is not soft either, so a limescale edge forms in both places. One sample is not an average.

Where does a rusty edge on the joint come from if there is little iron in the water?

In Jūrmala’s treated water in 2026 there was on average 9.7–16.0 µg/l of iron against a norm of 200 µg/l, so the cause is worth looking for elsewhere, for example in your own well water or in old pipes in the house.

Does repairing the floor under the bath in a historic summer house need permission?

In an urban planning monument area, the repair of a cultural monument or historic building that may alter the building may be carried out only with the written permission of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre. If the floor structure is changed, ask the centre before the work.
