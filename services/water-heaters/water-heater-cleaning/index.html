Water heater cleaning in a guest house - Legionella, limescale and hydrogen sulphide

Water heater cleaning in Jūrmala means draining the tank, removing the sediment and checking the anode.

A water heater clean-out with an anode check is from €75 per water heater, labour price, materials not included. For guest houses it is also part of limiting Legionella.

In May, before a guest house in Dzintari opens for the season

After the Second World War many large sanatoria and rest homes were built in Dzintari, and the area has a long history of receiving guests. For accommodation that takes guests, the ECDC 15-point plan has a clear recommendation: clean, drain and disinfect water heaters once a year.

The reason is at the bottom of the tank. ECDC explains that Legionella multiplies in standing water at 20–50 °C, including in water heaters, limescale and rubber seals. A water heater left unused over winter with sediment in it is exactly that kind of environment.

In the water of the Dzintari station the 2026 average values (as of 19 August 2026) show sulphates at 210 mg/l, the most of the four Jūrmala stations, against a norm of 250 mg/l. In a tank that has stood all winter, that is one more reason to open and clean the water heater before the first guests.

Nanofiltration, chlorine and testing: assumptions about Jūrmala's water

Often assumed: water in Jūrmala is soft because it is nanofiltered at the stations.

In fact: “Jūrmalas ūdens” (Jūrmala’s water utility) gives a total hardness of 3.21–4.25 mmol/l, and by a WHO document water above 1.8 mmol/l counts as very hard. The company does not plan to supply medium-hard or soft water, so additional softening is up to the consumers themselves.

Often assumed: chlorine in the water eats the anode quickly.

In fact: “Jūrmalas ūdens” does not add chlorine or fluorides to drinking water, and total iron in the treated water is 0.02–0.07 mg/l. How fast the anode wears depends on the composition of the water and how much hot water is used.

Often assumed: if “Jūrmalas ūdens” tests the water regularly, nothing can multiply in the water heater.

In fact: the routine samples sent to an accredited laboratory every month describe the water the city has treated and delivered, not water that has stood for months in your tank.

Water heater smells: sulphates and the anode, not the Ķemeri springs

Water Technology explains a smell in hot water by sulphate-reducing bacteria and hydrogen from the anode, not by the Ķemeri sulphur springs; more on this in the advice water smells of sulphur.

Between cleanings: the ECDC routine for a guest house

Water heaters are cleaned once a year, but water can stand in the pipes of an unoccupied room. ECDC recommends that accommodation appoints one person responsible for Legionella control and records water temperatures and other measurements every day.

  • In unoccupied rooms, at least once a week and before every guest arrival, all taps and showers are run for a few minutes until the water reaches the required temperature.
  • Hot water circulates throughout the system at 50–60 °C, and cold water stays below 20 °C right up to the outlets.
  • Shower heads and taps are kept free of limescale, because Legionella also multiplies in limescale and rubber seals.

For the log, a date, a room, the hot and cold water temperatures and a name are enough. If in some room the hot water takes a long time to reach 50 °C, that is a signal to check the water heater or the section of pipe leading to it.

ECDC guidance for owners of tourist accommodation on Legionella control, checked 19 November 2026.

Often asked in Jūrmala

How often should a water heater in a guest house be cleaned?

For tourist accommodation ECDC recommends cleaning, draining and disinfecting water heaters once a year. In a home the frequency is set by the amount of limescale seen when the tank is first opened; Jūrmala’s water is very hard, so it is worth not putting the cleaning off.

What chlorine concentration is used to disinfect a guest house's hot water system before the season?

The ECDC 15-point plan mentions 50 mg/l of chlorine for 2–4 hours before each season. It applies to the whole hot water system, not just the water heater; “Jūrmalas ūdens” does not add chlorine to drinking water itself.

Why is the Legionella risk greatest in the shower?

Data from the Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (SPKC) show that on average 33 cases of legionellosis a year are registered in Latvia. People are infected by breathing in a water aerosol, and in homes the greatest risk is in the shower, where the water is sprayed into tiny droplets.
