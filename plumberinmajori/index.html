Monuments No. 6087 and 6083 and the project brief

“Majoru–Dzintaru vasarnīcu kvartāls” is an urban planning monument (No. 6087), and Majori is also part of the monument of national importance No. 6083. The conditions of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre provide that, in urban planning monument areas, the reconstruction of a building needs a project brief. It is not needed for buildings without cultural and historical value or buildings built after 1940.

The Majori manor house at Konkordijas iela 66 (around 1910, architect W. Bockslaff) is an architectural monument of local importance. Article 21 of the law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments” provides that monuments of local importance may be repaired with municipal permission. If a plumbing job needs a new outlet through a wall or floor of such a building, usually ask the municipality first.

General administrative act of the Jūrmala Cultural Space and Environmental Design Centre of 5 June 2024 on the areas of urban planning monuments (likumi.lv, id 356995) and the law “On Protection of Cultural Monuments”, Article 21 (likumi.lv, id 72551), checked on 19 November 2026.

From the Turtle along Jomas iela to the Lielupe blocks

By the Sea Pavilion at the end of Tirgoņu iela stands Jānis Bārda’s bronze sculpture “The Turtle”, and in 1999 Majori beach was the first in Jūrmala to receive the Blue Flag. In an LSM report on the storm of 29 July 2024, Majori is named among the places where streets and yards flooded. Water on a street is a rainwater sewer matter: it is the responsibility of the Engineering Structures Division of the municipality’s Development Department, not of the domestic sewerage operator.

Jomas iela has been a pedestrian street since 1987, and its name comes from joma, the parallel dips in the dunes. The city plan for the whole of Jūrmala writes that dips between dunes typically have a high groundwater level, 0.5–2.0 m. This applies to the city as a whole, not to a measurement on a particular plot, but for a basement outlet and a yard trench in Majori it is worth keeping in mind.

South of the railway is a dense district of detached houses, with Soviet-era and new blocks of flats on the Lielupe bank and at the ends of J. Pliekšāna iela, Turaidas iela and Nomales iela. If there is a leak in such a block, Jūrmalas ūdens advises a private house owner to shut off the water to the particular room, or at the meter assembly for the whole building. In a flat this means: first the flat’s valve, then the neighbour below, then the form.

Majori's waste water and the phase IV streets

The Melluži sewage pumping station and rising main, built in the late 1960s and early 1970s, collected the waste water of Majori and Melluži and pumped it to the Sloka treatment plant. Jūrmala’s sewer is now 435.6 km long, with 55 pumping stations, so what goes into a toilet in Majori travels on through pumps.

Majori is also one of the places with the largest closed rainwater sewer systems in Jūrmala. The phase IV project included new network streets, for example Jaunā iela from Jūras iela to Jomas iela, and J. Pliekšāna iela and Viļuma iela between Jomas iela and Lienes iela. Whether the network on your street has already been built, Jūrmalas ūdens can say exactly.

A blockage and a leak in a Majori flat

Drain cleaning. “Clearing a blocked indoor drain” costs from €40 per job, and “Flushing the drains with water pressure” from €60, labour price, materials not included. If a blockage returns and the pipe is also flushed after clearing, together it is at least €100. In a block of flats you first find out whether the blockage is in the flat’s branch or in the shared riser.

Water leak. “Repairing a leaking spot on a pipe” costs from €35 per leak point; “Urgent visit while water is leaking” has no fixed price, and the price depends on night or weekend, distance and urgency. Jūrmalas ūdens gets involved in fixing faults only when the failure point is outside the territory adjoining the house, so a leak in the house is the owner’s or the building manager’s job. Two leak points in one flat are at least €70. In Majori the trip is included in the published labour prices.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Can wet wipes labelled “flushable” be put down a toilet in Majori?

No. At the Sloka waste water treatment plant, 14 tonnes of waste are separated out over a year, and wet wipes, even those labelled “flushable”, must go in the rubbish. On the way they can block both the house branch and the pump.

Can a toilet or shower be installed in the basement of a Majori house?

It can, but Jūrmala’s regulations (point 34.4) then require automatic shut-offs or gate valves on the sewer. If water has to be pumped up from a basement, Saniflo, for example, requires a loop in the rising main above the maximum backflow level, which is usually street level.

How often does the sewer in a Majori building need preventive cleaning?

Jūrmala’s regulations (point 34.5) require the user to clean the sewer networks in their own service zone preventively at least twice a year. In a block of flats, the building manager usually decides on the shared networks, unless the building’s documents say otherwise.
