Plumber in Smārde - private houses and four multi-storey blocks

Smārde is the centre of Smārde parish in Tukums Municipality, on both sides of the Riga-Tukums railway line.

Here the railway also divides the plumbing work: in the north there are mostly private houses with their own yard pipes, and in the south, next to private houses, stand four multi-storey blocks built in the Soviet period with shared risers.

Water and drains in Smārde: a shared inlet in the blocks, your own yard in private houses

In a multi-storey building, water arrives through the building’s inlet and risers, and these are usually looked after by the building manager: the manager knows who services the building’s network and where the riser shut-off valves are. The flat’s pipes beyond the shut-off valve, the taps and the water heater are usually yours to deal with, unless the building’s documents say otherwise.

A private house with its own sewerage falls under Tukums Municipality binding regulations No. 16 (23 February 2022): a septic tank has to be emptied at least yearly, and the register of decentralised systems is kept by the municipality’s water utility service provider. Which company serves your particular street can be seen on the water bill or in the contract, and you can ask the Tukums Municipality council.

More people live in Smārde than 50 years ago: 173 residents in 1970, 688 in 2000, 594 in 2025. In 2009–2021 Smārde was the centre of Engure Municipality, and since 2021 it has belonged to Tukums Municipality. Older house documents and contracts may therefore say Engure Municipality, but the sewerage procedure is now set by the Tukums Municipality regulations.

Tukums Municipality binding regulations No. 16 (23 February 2022) on the procedure for providing and recording decentralised sewerage services (likumi.lv), checked 19 November 2026.

From Smārde station to both sides of the railway

In the Brothers' Cemetery by the station stands the Kārlis Zāle monument to 38 soldiers killed in the First World War, unveiled on 21 June 1936. Smārde was first mentioned in 1253, and the village grew first around Smārde manor and later around the railway station, so its parts were built at different times. The year a house was built tells the plumber which pipes to expect in the walls, so it is worth writing it in the request.

North of the railway there are mostly private houses. Each has its own service pipe from the street or its own system, and the yard pipe is longer than for a multi-storey building. A leak in such a pipe often does not show in the house: it appears as a wet patch on the lawn or as a meter that turns when all the taps are closed.

South of the tracks stand private houses and four multi-storey residential buildings built in the Soviet period. In them one riser usually serves all the flats in one vertical line, so a leak on an upper floor usually appears in the ceiling of the flat below, not where it starts. Repairing a leaking spot on a pipe: from €35 per leak point.

Smārde parish has two railway stations, Smārde and Milzkalne, as well as the Milzukalns Nature Park, part of Ķemeri National Park and a small part of Lake Kaņieris, and the Slocene crosses the parish. In the request write whether the house is in Smārde village itself by the station, because the parish is large and the road to the house differs.

Risers in blocks and pipes in private houses in Smārde

Riser replacement. In the four multi-storey buildings south of the railway, water and drainage run through risers across all floors, and a riser can be replaced floor by floor, without changing it all at once. Water riser replaced on one floor: from €120 per floor. Soil stack replaced on one floor: from €120 per floor. If a water riser is replaced on two floors, the work is at least €240, materials not included. When the riser can be shut off, you agree with the building manager.

Replacing pipes in a private house. In private houses north of the tracks, pipes can be replaced section by section or the whole system at once. Replacing water pipe: from €10/m, so a 15 m section is at least €150 of labour. Repiping a whole flat or house: from €500 per property. Both prices are labour prices, materials not included. If a yard pipe lies shallower than the frost depth (see the question below), it is worth laying it deeper at the same time as replacing the pipes in the house.

Smārde lies outside Jūrmala, 12 km from Tukums. Even so, getting to Smārde is included in the labour price.

Often asked in Jūrmala

Our flat is in the old Smārde school building. Why might the pipes run differently here than in neighbouring buildings?

The old Smārde primary school building opened in 1924 as a school and was only later converted into flats; the new school opened in 1975. Water and drainage for the flats in such a building were most likely put in during the conversion, not to a standard design. During the call it helps to know on which floor the flat is and where the pipes are visible.

A private house has its own well and also a water main connection. May they be joined?

Not directly. LBN 222-15 (point 150) forbids joining a drinking water system with a system whose water does not meet drinking water requirements. We do not service well pumps, but the house’s pipes can be rebuilt so that the two sources stay separate.

How deep must a water pipe lie in a Smārde yard so that it does not freeze?

For example, for the Riga station LBN 003-19 gives, for clayey soil, 59 cm (recurring once in two years) and 91 cm (once in ten years). The value for sandy soil is 1.2 times larger: about 71 cm and about 109 cm. For the Smārde site see the value in the table for the nearest station; a pipe that lies shallower has to be insulated or drained in winter.
